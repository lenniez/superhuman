Coordination on the go

Tasks arrive all day by call, email and text, as users jump between meetings and often while on the go

FrequencyHighSeverityHigh
  • Project planning: creating up-front, end-to-end project plans with owners of each step is complex, detailed, and time-consuming.
  • Daily morning triage: important requests are scattered across email, texts, DMs and voicemails, so urgent items get buried and it takes time to figure out what needs attention.
  • Coordination on the go: commitments made mid-call or between appointments aren't captured well or are disorganized, because there's little time to write them down. Promised follow-ups may get forgotten.
  • Getting paid: invoicing, payment chasing and paperwork get pushed to evenings or delayed, slowing cash flow and eating into personal time, causing burnout.
  • Prospecting (ongoing): lead follow-ups and business development fall through the cracks when client work spikes, creating feast-or-famine income cycles.
Potential solutions

Three solutions explored

Solution 1
Cross-channel task collector & list generator
Agent parses conversations and generates a task list in real time
EmailPhoneVideoLiveTextsSlackTeams
Solution 2
One-press voice / text capture
Action Button or shortcut → speak → captured, no unlocking
  • Enriched with calendar and location
  • Prompts for due date, collaborators
Solution 3
Agent-owned task category
Agent listens for preset, approved tasks and handles them
  • Simple email / message responses
  • Scheduling meetings
  • 1. Cross-channel task collector & list generator: agent integrates with cross-channel discussions and generates / updates the task list in real time. Parses email, phone, video or live conversation transcripts, texts, or Slack/Teams to gather tasks, deadlines, and roughly prioritize. Generates a suggested board (Backlog, To Do Today, In Progress, Blocked, Done) or calendar view the user can interact with.
  • 2. One-press voice/text capture: map the iPhone Action Button, Android shortcut, or laptop shortcut to instant voice capture. One press, speak the tasks, captured, without unlocking or opening apps. Enriched with recent calendar invites or location; user may be prompted for due date, collaborators, requesters; tasks can be sent to Trello, Notion, documents.
  • 3. Agent-owned task category: agent listens to specific channels for preset tasks it can handle independently and that the user has approved, minimizing tasks the user needs to track: simple email/message responses, scheduling meetings.

Keeper’s AI touchpoints

Phase 1LLM creates & updates to‑do list
  1. 1Captures

    Reads every channel, separates real tasks from basic replies, names each task and saves the source snippet for back-linking.

  2. 2Organizes

    Auto-categorizes every task by project, requester, due date and channel.

  3. 3Updates

    On later passes, detects full or partial completion and context changes like a new due date, and updates the task.

Phase 2Agent handles simple work
  1. 4Drafts replies

    Uses the conversation and the documents you train it on.

  2. 5Checks availability

    Reads connected calendars to find open time.

  3. 6Schedules

    Handles the back-and-forth and sends the calendar invite.

  4. 7With permission

    Permissions are set by prompts and your settings.

Design rationale

01
Built for the car, the hallway and the kitchen, not just the desk.

Mobile first: one status at a time, swipe to finish, and voice capture for hands-free moments.

02
Builds trust before automation.

Every task links to its source and new tasks wait for review. The agent drafts before it ever sends.

03
It’s a to-do list, not a complex workflow tool.

Up Next, In Progress, Done. The product should feel like a list in a notebook.

04
Borrowed what works from competitors.

Superhuman Mail’s response drafts, Otter’s backlinking to source content, ChatGPT’s voice mode, Todoist’s simplicity.

05
Color usage is warm, human and specific.

Teal for actions, marigold for anything that needs you, and red only when something is overdue.

06
The name: Keeper.

Like a goalie, it makes sure nothing gets past it, and it keeps your tasks for you.

  • Built for the car, the hallway and the kitchen, not just the desk. Mobile first: one status at a time, swipe to finish, and voice capture for hands-free moments.
  • Builds trust before automation. Every task links to its source and new tasks wait for review. The agent drafts before it ever sends.
  • It’s a to-do list, not a complex workflow tool. Up Next, In Progress, Done. The product should feel like a list in a notebook.
  • Borrowed what works from competitors. Superhuman Mail’s response drafts, Otter’s backlinking to source content, ChatGPT’s voice mode, Todoist’s simplicity.
  • Color usage is warm, human and specific. Teal for actions, marigold for anything that needs you, and red only when something is overdue.
  • The name: Keeper. Like a goalie, it makes sure nothing gets past it, and it keeps your tasks for you.

Rationale

  • Why not B2B first: crowded, feature-rich competitors and long sales motions
  • Market progression: start with prosumers, then migrate to SMB, then Enterprise, where revenue opportunities are far greater
  • Why not pure consumer: consumer productivity apps hit a revenue ceiling
  • Solving for general task management, using AI to solve and as a differentiator.
  • 0-1 build, new startup; assume some seed funding exists.
  • Start B2C prosumer, expecting it to serve as a wedge into SMB and then Enterprise, which hold far greater revenue opportunities.
  • Why: early-stage startup with limited funding; tough to immediately enter a crowded B2B space with feature-rich competitors and long, complex sales motions.
  • Why not pure consumer: consumer productivity apps have a ceiling and are hard to scale revenue.
  • B2C is also generally more underserved in task management using AI than B2B.
  • Begin in the US market (most familiar to our team), then scale globally.
